# Content Strategy Memo — Netflix Catalog

**To:** Content Acquisition & Strategy Team  
**From:** OTT Content Analytics  
**Subject:** What the public Netflix catalog can—and cannot—tell us  
**Date:** 19 November 2026

## Executive summary
The attached analysis turns a public Netflix catalog snapshot into a supply-side view of format, genre, release-year, maturity-rating, production-country and duration patterns. It is useful for mapping the shape of the catalog and forming acquisition questions. It is not a measure of what audiences watched or what made money: the source does not include viewing hours, completion, retention, subscriber demand, licensing cost or title-level performance.

## Three actionable recommendations

### 1. Make genre investment decisions using a supply-versus-demand scorecard
Use the notebook's top-genre and genre-by-release-year tables to identify heavily represented categories and possible catalog gaps. Do not automatically invest more in the most common genres. Join these supply counts to internal watch hours, completion rate, subscriber acquisition/retention contribution and cost per viewing hour. Prioritize genres where audience demand is strong and the current catalog is comparatively thin; test small slates before committing to large multi-title deals.

**Next action:** Build a genre scorecard with catalog count, viewing hours, completion, retention lift and acquisition cost; review quarterly.

### 2. Turn country representation into a localized acquisition pipeline
The country chart highlights production geographies represented in the catalog. Use it to shortlist markets with a meaningful audience opportunity and relatively low catalog representation. Validate with local subscriber growth, language preferences, regional viewing data, rights availability, production capacity and regulatory requirements. Multi-country titles appear in more than one country count, so these figures are associations rather than exclusive market shares.

**Next action:** Select three candidate markets for a deeper local-demand and rights review; commission a small, measurable pilot slate only after validation.

### 3. Design a deliberate mix of movies and series formats
The catalog format split, movie runtime distribution and TV season distribution can inform slate planning. Maintain a mix of movie lengths and series commitments rather than assuming one format wins. Test shorter films, limited series and longer-running shows against segment-level completion and repeat viewing. A title's runtime or season count is a production attribute, not a quality or success signal.

**Next action:** Run a format test plan with clearly defined audience segments and compare completion, return viewing and cost efficiency.

## How to use the notebook
The notebook generates eight charts: catalog mix, release-year trend, genre mix, rating profile, country mix, movie runtime and TV season distributions, additions by year and format, and genre trends over time. It also exports cleaned records and summary tables for follow-up analysis.

## Important limitations
- The source is a historical Netflix catalog snapshot, not a live 2026 catalog.
- It contains Netflix only; it does not support direct Prime Video or Disney+ Hotstar comparisons.
- It describes catalog metadata, not audience behavior or commercial performance.
- Missing values are retained as `Unknown` in categorical analysis where practical.
- Multi-genre and multi-country titles are counted in every associated category.
- Original release year and platform-added year answer different questions.

## Decision
Use this work to frame acquisition hypotheses and prioritize follow-up questions—not as a standalone greenlight recommendation. Before investment decisions, combine the catalog view with first-party audience data, market research, rights economics and comparable platform catalogs.
